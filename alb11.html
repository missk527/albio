ALB11. Enzyme Action

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

What Are Enzymes?
  • Enzymes are globular proteins that act as biological catalysts in living organisms.
  • They speed up chemical reactions without being used up and can be reused.
Activation Energy
  • Activation energy is the initial energy needed to start a reaction.
  • Enzymes lower the activation-energy barrier, so reactions happen more easily and quickly.
  • On an energy profile, the catalysed reaction has a lower peak relative to the reactants.
Intracellular and Extracellular Enzymes
  • Intracellular enzymes act inside cells. Catalase breaks down harmful hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.
  • Extracellular enzymes act outside cells, even when they are still inside the body.
  • Amylase acts in the mouth and small intestine, breaking starch into smaller carbohydrates.
  • Trypsin acts in the small intestine, breaking proteins into smaller peptides. Further digestion produces amino acids.
Active Sites and Enzyme Action
  • An enzyme has a specific three-dimensional shape called its tertiary structure.
  • The active site is the region where a substrate binds. A substrate is the molecule the enzyme acts on.
  • A complementary active site fits its substrate, helping explain enzyme specificity.
  • Binding forms an enzyme-substrate complex. Temporary interactions help lower activation energy.
  • Products are released after the reaction; the enzyme remains unchanged and can bind another substrate.
Lock and Key Model
  • The active site has a fixed shape that is complementary to a particular substrate.
  • The substrate fits precisely, like a key fitting a lock.
Induced Fit Model
  • The active site is flexible; the substrate does not fit perfectly at first.
  • Substrate binding causes a slight change in active-site shape, improving the fit.
  • This change can strain bonds in the substrate and lower activation energy.
  • The video explains that there is more supporting evidence for induced fit than for the lock and key model.
