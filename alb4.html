ALB4. Testing for Carbohydrates

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Introduction | Carbohydrate Tests
  • Food tests can identify reducing sugars, non-reducing sugars, and starch.
  • Each test uses a specific reagent and a characteristic colour change.
Reducing and Non-reducing Sugars
  • Reducing sugars donate electrons and reduce other molecules.
  • All monosaccharides, including glucose, fructose, and galactose, are reducing sugars.
  • Some disaccharides, such as maltose and lactose, are also reducing sugars.
  • Most disaccharides, including sucrose, are non-reducing; polysaccharides such as glycogen and cellulose are non-reducing carbohydrates.
  • “Sugar” refers to simple carbohydrates: monosaccharides and disaccharides.
Benedict’s Test | Reducing Sugars
  • Add 2 cm3 of the food sample and 2 cm3 of blue Benedict’s reagent to a test tube.
  • Heat the mixture for 5 minutes in a gently boiling water bath.
  • A blue solution is a negative result: no reducing sugar is detected.
  • A colour change is positive. Green indicates a low concentration, orange a medium concentration, and brick red a high concentration.
Testing for Non-reducing Sugars
  • First carry out Benedict’s test. Continue only if the result is negative.
  • Place a fresh 2 cm3 sample in another test tube, add 2 cm3 of dilute hydrochloric acid, and heat for 5 minutes.
  • Acid hydrolysis breaks non-reducing sugars into reducing sugars.
  • Add sodium hydrogen carbonate to neutralise the acid, then repeat Benedict’s test.
  • A positive result, such as a brick-red precipitate, shows that the original sample contained non-reducing sugar.
Measuring Sugar Concentration
  • A colorimeter measures how much light a solution absorbs.
  • Low absorbance suggests a low reducing-sugar concentration; high absorbance suggests a higher concentration.
  • Alternatively, filter and weigh the precipitate: a greater precipitate mass indicates more sugar.
Iodine Test | Starch
  • Put 2 cm3 of the food sample into a test tube, add a few drops of orange iodine solution, and shake.
  • If the mixture stays orange, the result is negative and starch is absent.
  • If it changes to blue-black, the result is positive and starch is present.
Summary | Interpreting Results
  • Benedict’s reagent tests for reducing sugars after heating.
  • Acid hydrolysis, neutralisation, and a second Benedict’s test reveal non-reducing sugars.
  • Iodine solution tests for starch without a water bath.
