ALB7. Biological Molecules

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

The Four Main Types of Biological Molecules
  • The four major groups are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids.
  • They perform essential structural, energy-storage and information-related roles in living organisms.
Carbon and Organic Molecules
  • Biological molecules are organic because they contain carbon.
  • Carbon can form four covalent bonds with carbon or other elements.
  • Its single and double bonds allow a wide variety of complex structures.
  • Covalent bonds are strong because atoms share electrons.
Elemental Composition
  • All four groups contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.
  • Carbohydrates contain only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.
  • Proteins always contain nitrogen, while nucleic acids always contain nitrogen and phosphorus.
  • Lipids, proteins and nucleic acids may contain additional elements.
Monomers
  • Monomers are small basic units that can join to make larger structures.
  • Carbohydrate monomers are monosaccharides, such as glucose.
  • Protein monomers are amino acids.
  • Nucleic-acid monomers are nucleotides.
Polymers and Polymerisation
  • Polymers are large molecules made from many repeating monomer subunits.
  • Polysaccharides, such as glycogen, are carbohydrate polymers made from monosaccharides.
  • Polypeptides are protein polymers made from amino acids.
  • Polynucleotides, including DNA strands, are long chains of nucleotides.
  • Polymerisation is the joining of monomers to form polymers.
Why Lipids Are Not Polymers
  • Lipids contain subunits such as glycerol and fatty acids.
  • However, these subunits do not form long chains of repeating units.
  • Therefore, lipids do not technically meet the definition of polymers.
Condensation Reactions
  • Most polymers are synthesised through condensation reactions.
  • A hydroxyl group from one molecule and a hydrogen from another are removed.
  • This forms a chemical bond and releases one molecule of water.
  • The reaction requires energy supplied by ATP.
  • Each amino acid added to a polypeptide releases one water molecule.
Hydrolysis Reactions
  • Hydrolysis breaks polymers and other large molecules, including lipids, into subunits.
  • Water is split to provide H and OH groups that break a chemical bond.
  • Hydrolysis releases energy in the process described in the video.
  • For example, a polypeptide can be hydrolysed into amino acids.
