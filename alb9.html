ALB9. Water

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Overview | Structure and Functions of Water
  • Water is essential to living organisms because of its molecular structure and polarity.
  • Its major roles include acting as a solvent, metabolite, transport medium, temperature regulator, and habitat.
Water Molecule | Covalent Bonds and Polarity
  • One water molecule contains one oxygen atom covalently bonded to two hydrogen atoms.
  • The atoms share electrons, but oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly.
  • Oxygen therefore has a partial negative charge (δ−), while each hydrogen has a partial positive charge (δ+).
  • This unequal charge distribution makes water dipolar, with positive and negative poles.
Hydrogen Bonding | Attraction Between Molecules
  • The partially positive hydrogen of one water molecule attracts the partially negative oxygen of another.
  • This attraction forms a hydrogen bond.
  • Hydrogen bonds are weak individually but strong collectively, causing water molecules to stay together.
Water as a Solvent | Dissolving Ionic Compounds
  • Ionic compounds contain positively charged cations and negatively charged anions.
  • In water, cations are attracted to δ− oxygen atoms and anions to δ+ hydrogen atoms.
  • This separates and surrounds the ions, allowing many ionic substances to dissolve.
  • Water is often called a universal solvent because it dissolves many substances.
Biological Solutions | Plasma and Cytoplasm
  • Blood plasma is mostly water, so it can transport many dissolved substances around the body.
  • Many important biological reactions occur in solution within the cytoplasm.
Water as a Metabolite | Reactions
  • Water is directly involved in many metabolic reactions.
  • Hydrolysis uses water to break large molecules into smaller ones.
  • Condensation joins molecules and releases water.
  • Water is a reactant in photosynthesis and a product of respiration.
Transport Medium | Cohesion and Adhesion
  • Cohesion is the attraction between water molecules caused by hydrogen bonding.
  • Adhesion is the attraction between water molecules and other polar surfaces.
  • In xylem, adhesion helps water cling to vessel walls, while cohesion maintains a continuous water column.
  • Together, these properties help move water and dissolved minerals upward through plants.
Surface Tension | Supporting Small Organisms
  • Strong cohesion creates high surface tension where water meets air.
  • The water surface behaves like a thin skin and can support small organisms such as water striders.
Temperature Control | Heat Capacity and Evaporation
  • Water has a high specific heat capacity because much energy is needed to disrupt its many hydrogen bonds.
  • It absorbs substantial energy before its temperature rises, helping organisms maintain stable temperatures.
  • Water also has a high latent heat of vaporization, so evaporation removes a large amount of heat.
  • Sweating cools the body effectively without requiring the loss of excessive water.
Water as a Habitat | Stability and Floating Ice
  • Water's thermal properties help aquatic habitats maintain a relatively stable temperature and physical state.
  • When water freezes, hydrogen bonds hold molecules farther apart than in liquid water.
  • Ice is therefore less dense than liquid water and floats.
  • Floating ice insulates the water below, reducing further freezing and protecting aquatic life in winter.
